# Sales Variance Analysis: Insights & Recommendations

**Scope:** 25,920 sales lines · 5 regions · 5 categories · 27 products · Jan 2024 – Dec 2025 (24 months) · synthetic portfolio dataset

## 1. Executive Overview

Sales finished **$9.47M (1.94%) below target**: $478.75M actual against $488.21M planned, with quantity attainment of 98.03% (81,248 units short). The company-wide miss looks mild, but it hides two very different businesses. **Asia Pacific beat target by $4.48M (+4.57%)**; the other four regions missed, and **Latin America alone accounts for 74% of the net shortfall** from 19.8% of the target. Every month of the 24 was below target, and the gap did not close in the second year (–1.92% in 2024, –1.96% in 2025). The variance is **100% volume: the price effect is exactly $0**. In margin terms the shortfall cost roughly **$3.27M of gross profit** (about 2% of the $165.4M earned).

## 2. Key Insights

### 1. The company-level miss is small, stable and not improving
A –1.94% gap is easy to dismiss, but it is $9.47M of revenue and about $3.27M of gross margin (gross margin runs at a steady 34.5%). The business itself is flat: 2025 actual sales grew just +0.11% on 2024, and targets grew +0.15%. Year two repeated year one almost to the basis point (–1.92% then –1.96%), with no visible sign that the first-year miss changed how targets were set. The monthly gap stays in a narrow band, from –1.17% (Jul 2024) to –2.87% (Oct 2025), and the weakest quarters were Q4 2025 (–2.29%) and Q2 2025 (–2.27%). The latest quarter is not an improvement.

### 2. The problem is regional, and it is Latin America
| Region | Variance | Variance % | Share of target | Lines below target | Months below target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Asia Pacific | +$4,478,728 | +4.57% | 20.1% | 36% | 0 of 24 |
| Europe | –$2,199,064 | –2.22% | 20.2% | 56% | 24 of 24 |
| North America | –$2,342,136 | –2.40% | 20.0% | 56% | 24 of 24 |
| Middle East | –$2,379,765 | –2.45% | 19.9% | 55% | 24 of 24 |
| Latin America | –$7,022,791 | –7.26% | 19.8% | 68% | 24 of 24 |

- Latin America carries **74% of the net shortfall (–$7.02M)** on 19.8% of the target. Remove it and the rest of the company is only –$2.44M (–0.62%).
- It is broad, not a pocket: **none of its 27 products beat target** (range –3.72% to –9.81%), 68% of its sales lines miss (Asia Pacific: 36%), and it did not improve (sales attainment 92.85% in 2024, 92.63% in 2025). That looks like a market-level or structural issue, not a product or execution blip.
- If Latin America had missed at the rate of Europe, North America and the Middle East combined (–2.36%), the company would have recovered about **$4.74M, roughly 50% of the total gap** (about $1.6M of gross margin).
- Europe, North America and the Middle East are almost identical (–2.22% to –2.45%), which points to a common cause (e.g. how targets are set) rather than three separate local problems.
- Asia Pacific is the offset: without its $4.48M surplus the other four regions are –$13.94M (–3.57%) below target.

### 3. Categories: Electronics is the only winner, and only because of Asia Pacific
- **Electronics** is the only category above target (+$960,846, +0.89%). But Asia Pacific Electronics alone delivered +$1,643,968; **outside Asia Pacific, Electronics is –$683,122 (–0.79%) below target.** The category result is a regional result in disguise.
- **Home Appliances** has the largest dollar gap (–$2,870,916, –2.64%) and is the only category that is clearly getting worse: attainment fell from 98.01% to 96.72% between 2024 and 2025, deteriorating in 4 of 5 regions. **Personal Care** is actually the worst in percentage terms (–2.96%, –$2,700,685); Office Supplies (–2.60%) and Sports Equipment (–2.78%) are close behind. All four non-Electronics categories sit in a tight –2.60% to –2.96% band, so this is a portfolio-wide pattern with Home Appliances as the sharpest edge.
- Take Asia Pacific and Electronics out of the picture and the remaining business is **–$13.26M (–4.37%)** below target, equivalent to 140% of the net variance.

### 4. It is a volume problem, but the data cannot rule out a pricing problem
The price effect is **$0.00 on every one of 25,920 lines**, so the full $9.47M variance is formally volume. Treat that with care: actual unit prices equal the planned price (about $118.56 on average) on every line, even though 112 lines carry a "Price change" note. That strongly suggests the price field is a **standard/list price, not the price customers actually paid**, so the data show no price variance because they cannot show one. Discounting, promotions and mix may be hiding inside the volume number. What can be said is that units are the issue: 81,248 fewer units were sold than planned (98.03% attainment).

### 5. The under-delivery is systematic, and the target-setting evidence points to bias
- All 24 months missed target, but the stronger fact is regional: **each of the four non-Asia Pacific regions missed in 24 of 24 months, and Asia Pacific beat target in 24 of 24.** That is not random variation.
- The tracking signal of –24 should be read carefully: when every month is below target it is arithmetically –(number of months), so it is the mathematical floor and restates the 24-of-24 fact rather than adding a second proof.
- Targets look **allocated, not forecast**: each region carries 19.8% to 20.2% of total target (almost exactly one-fifth), yet attainment ranges from 92.7% (Latin America) to 104.6% (Asia Pacific), an 11.8-point spread. That is the pattern you would expect if targets were split evenly across markets whose real capacity differs.
- The 2025 target was set **2.1% above what the business actually delivered in 2024**. With a flat business, a miss of about 2% was effectively built in.
- Honest limit: this data shows that targets and results are structurally disconnected; it cannot say whether targets were too high (bias) or execution was too weak. For Asia Pacific, which always beats target, the likelier story is targets that are too low.

### 6. Chronic shortfalls: 17 of 25 Region × Category pairs miss in at least 75% of months
- All four Latin America pairs outside Electronics missed in **24 of 24 months**, and Latin America Electronics in 22 of 24. Europe, North America and the Middle East each miss in 20 to 23 months in every category except Electronics. Only Asia Pacific (0 to 4 misses per category) and the Electronics lines of the other three regions escape.
- The five largest gaps of all 25 pairs are the five Latin America categories, led by **Latin America × Home Appliances at –$1,779,783 (–8.51%, 19% of the whole company gap)**.
- At monthly Region × Category level, 423 of 600 cells (70%) are below target. Only 57 cells breach the materiality rules, and they concentrate where you would expect: 37 in Latin America, 14 in Asia Pacific (all favourable) and 6 elsewhere.

### 7. Product shortfalls are broad, not concentrated
Only 6 of 27 products beat target, and all 6 are Electronics; the other 21 miss, which together add up to a –$10.43M gross product shortfall that the winners offset by only 9% (+$960,846). The worst three are **Face Massager (–$657,081, –3.67%), Webcam (–$630,675, –3.48%) and Trimmer (–$615,826, –3.42%)**, but together they explain only 18% of the shortfall, and it takes 16 products to reach 80%. There is no small set of SKUs that fixes this. Operational incidents are not the cause either: the 699 lines carrying an operational note (Damaged, Late shipment, Return, Stock issue, Promo, Price change) account for just –$335,178, or 3.5% of the net variance.

## 3. Recommendations (in priority order)

**1. Launch a Latin America recovery review (start now, 30 to 90 days).** Run a root-cause diagnostic on the market, channel and distribution position in the four categories that missed every month, starting with Home Appliances (–$1,779,783) and Personal Care. Agree an interim recovery target and a named owner. *Prize:* closing the gap to the peer-region level is worth about $4.74M of revenue ($1.6M gross margin). *Success measure (proposed):* take the Latin America variance from –7.26% to about –4.8% within two quarters, and stop the 24-of-24 monthly miss streak.

**2. Rebuild target-setting (next planning cycle, structural).** Replace near-equal regional allocation with bottom-up, capacity-based targets that start from trailing attainment, and require any target above last year's actual to carry a written growth rationale. On trailing results Asia Pacific delivers about 4.6% more than its target while Latin America delivers about 7.3% less; the aim is realistic allocation, not lower ambition. Do not simply cut Latin America's target before recommendation 1 has diagnosed the cause. *Success measure:* tracking signal inside ±4 and regional attainment within a few points of 100%.

**3. Open a Home Appliances and Personal Care commercial review (this quarter).** Home Appliances is the largest dollar gap and the only category deteriorating (attainment 98.0% to 96.7%), and Personal Care is the weakest in percentage terms. Review assortment, availability and pricing position by region at category level, rather than chasing individual SKUs, because the shortfall is spread across 16 products. Use Face Massager, Webcam and Trimmer as test cases. *Success measure:* Home Appliances attainment back above its 2024 level of 98.0%.

**4. Capture the Asia Pacific playbook and re-stretch its target (this quarter).** Asia Pacific beat target in all 24 months and in every category (Electronics at +7.71%). Document what it does differently in range, channel and execution, test what transfers to Latin America and to Electronics elsewhere (currently –0.79%), and reset its target to a realistic stretch so the surplus is no longer masking weakness elsewhere in the consolidated number.

**5. Upgrade variance reporting so management sees causes, not noise (next 60 days).** (a) Capture the **actual transaction price** (net of discounts and promotions) so price and mix can finally be measured; today the answer is a structural $0. (b) Replace line-level flags, which mark 50% of lines as material because individual lines typically swing by about ±15%, with tiered thresholds at Region × Category × Month level: that produces 57 exceptions instead of 12,968. (c) Add a rolling tracking-signal and a one-page monthly bridge (target to volume to price to actual) to the management pack.

## 4. Closing Note

This is a **synthetic dataset**, and it shows: price never varies from plan, Asia Pacific beats target every single month while the other four regions miss every single month, and the gap barely moves between years. Real data would be noisier and would almost certainly show price and mix effects. The value of the project is the method, not the business story: raw data was cleaned (1,795 lines had quantities repaired; excluding them the variance is –1.97%, so the conclusions do not depend on the repairs), every classification was computed in exact cents so a line sitting exactly on a threshold is not misjudged, price and volume effects reconcile to the variance to the cent, and every table ties back to the grand totals. Causes named above are hypotheses to test, not findings; the data contains no market, competitor, customer or cost-variance information.
