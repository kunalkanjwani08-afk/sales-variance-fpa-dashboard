> **Synthetic data.** The figures come from a simulated business generated by `scripts/generate_synthetic_data.py`. They illustrate the method; they do not describe a real company. This file is generated by `scripts/build_insights.py` from the current analysis outputs - edit the script, not this file.

**Scope:** 20,900 sales lines · 5 regions · 5 categories · 27 products · 2024-01 to 2025-12 (24 months).

## 1. Executive summary

Sales finished **$10.9M (2.04%) below target** and gross profit **$13.3M (6.53%) below target**. Gross margin moved from 38.18% planned to 36.43%. Lower selling prices, not lower volume, explain the revenue miss: the price effect is **–$11.2M** (average selling price –2.09% vs target) while units reached 99.73% of plan. Unit costs above target took a further **$2.1M** of gross profit, so profit fell faster than sales. 20 of 24 months finished below target.

The result is not uniform. **Latin America** is the weakest region (–10.65%) and accounts for 64% of the net shortfall; **Asia Pacific** is the strongest (+4.15%).

## 2. What the data shows

### 2.1 Discounts bought volume, but not profit
- 4,063 lines (19% of all lines) were sold at least 5% below their target price. They reached **112.11% of planned units** and +3.39% of target sales, but **–16.04% of target gross profit**.
- All other lines reached 96.78% of planned units and –4.23% of target gross profit.
- Discounted lines carry $11.0M of the $11.2M total price effect.
- *Evidence vs hypothesis:* the data shows that deeper discounts came with more units and less profit. It cannot show whether those units were incremental or pulled forward from other months or other products (cannibalisation) - that needs a baseline-demand estimate.

### 2.2 Two regional problems with different causes
- **Latin America** (–$7.0M, –10.65%) is a **volume** problem: units reached 90.78% of plan and pure volume explains –$6.1M; its selling prices were –2.08% vs target, close to the company average.
  - It is broad, not one product: 0 of 27 products beat target there.
  - In 2025 its monthly targets missed actual sales by 16.48% on average (WAPE), while a naive forecast (the same month of 2024) missed by only 3.70%. The target was further from reality than last year's actuals.
- **Europe** has the weakest pricing: selling prices –2.75% vs target (price effect –$3.1M) and gross profit –10.46%.
  - In 2025, when list prices were planned to rise, Europe realised –3.49% vs target against –2.20% to –1.27% in the other regions.
  - Its 2025 target error (WAPE 8.01%) is also larger than a naive forecast (2.55%).
- **Asia Pacific** (+$5.4M, +4.15%) beat target on volume (106.04% of planned units) despite selling 1.58% below target price.

### 2.3 Home Appliances: a cost problem on top of price
- Home Appliances has the largest gross-profit shortfall in percentage terms (–14.92%, –$5.2M) although its sales variance (–1.77%) is close to the other categories.
- Unit costs above target took –$2.6M of its gross profit; from 2025-03 onwards its unit costs ran 4% or more above target. Its gross margin fell from 33.96% planned to 29.41%.
- By contrast, Electronics costs came in below target and added +$1.0M of gross profit.
- *Evidence vs hypothesis:* the cost increase is measured; whether it came from freight, components or supplier pricing cannot be told without a cost breakdown.

### 2.4 Timing: the holiday peak was under-planned
- Only 4 of 24 months beat target: 2024-11, 2024-12, 2025-11, 2025-12.
- The target spread each year more evenly than sales really came in: in 2024-12 actual sales were 9.84% of the year against 9.10% planned, while in 2025-07 they were 7.34% against 7.80%.
- In the months that beat target, selling prices were –4.13% vs target, against –1.60% in the other months: part of the peak-month gain was bought with discounts.
- The largest negative exceptions are North America Electronics in 2024-08, 2024-09, 2024-10 (–24.8% to –31.6%): a short, sharp drop rather than a trend. 63 of the 115 lines in those months carry a 'Stock-out' note, which points to supply rather than demand.

### 2.5 Persistence and products
- **13 of 25** region × category pairs were below target in at least 75% of months (Europe: 5, Latin America: 5, North America: 3). Repeated misses point to something structural - the target, the market or pricing - rather than one-off events.
- **6 of 27** products beat target. The shortfall is spread out: 13 products make up 80% of it, so a product-by-product fix would miss the regional and pricing causes above.
- 2025 was weaker than 2024: sales –2.31% vs –1.75% and gross profit –7.45% vs –5.54%.

## 3. What follows from the method, not from the business

- **Mix depends on the grouping.** The mix effect values selling a different combination of lines (products, regions, accounts) at target prices. It is calculated within whatever group is being viewed, so the company mix and the sum of regional mixes differ.
- **The tracking signal restates the monthly pattern.** It is cumulative variance divided by the average monthly miss. With 20 of 24 months below target it is strongly negative by construction; it is a warning light, not separate proof of target bias.
- **Line-level materiality is noisy.** 10,526 lines (50%) meet the materiality rule because single accounts swing a lot from month to month. The region × category × month exceptions list (279 cells, largest first) is the place to start.
- **Status and materiality are separate.** A group can be within the ±3% tolerance and still material in dollars - the company total is an example.

## 4. Recommendations

Each recommendation states what was observed, what it is worth, what might be causing it (a hypothesis), what to do, how to measure success, and what data would confirm the cause. In a real business these would be validated with the commercial teams first.

### R1. Put a profit test on discounts
- **Observed:** lines sold 5% or more below target price reached 112.11% of planned units but –16.04% of target gross profit.
- **Impact:** the price effect is –$11.2M for the period; every dollar of discount falls straight to gross profit.
- **Possible mechanism (hypothesis):** promotions are deeper than the extra volume can pay for, especially in lower-margin categories.
- **Action:** approve promotions against a gross-profit payback (extra units × unit margin ≥ discount cost), with a floor price by category.
- **Success measure:** average selling price within 1% of target and discounted lines at or above target gross profit for two consecutive quarters (today –2.09% and –16.04%).
- **Data needed:** promotion calendar and mechanics, baseline (non-promoted) demand, competitor prices.

### R2. Re-base the Latin America plan and diagnose demand
- **Observed:** Latin America sold 90.78% of planned units, its 2025 targets were less accurate than a naive forecast, and the shortfall is spread across all products.
- **Impact:** –$7.0M of sales and –$3.2M of gross profit.
- **Possible mechanism (hypothesis):** the target assumed growth the market did not deliver, and/or distribution or demand is weaker than planned. The data cannot separate the two.
- **Action:** set the next Latin America targets from trailing actuals plus an explicit, owned growth assumption; run a separate demand and distribution review.
- **Success measure:** monthly target error (WAPE) below the naive forecast and variance within ±3% (today 16.48% and –10.65%).
- **Data needed:** market size and share, distribution points, competitor activity.

### R3. Recover the Europe price increase
- **Observed:** Europe realised –2.75% vs target price and –3.49% in 2025.
- **Impact:** price effect –$3.1M; gross profit –10.46% vs target.
- **Possible mechanism (hypothesis):** planned list-price increases were not fully passed on (contract terms, customer resistance or local competition).
- **Action:** review pass-through by customer and contract; agree a phased recovery plan where increases were waived.
- **Success measure:** Europe selling price within 1% of target.
- **Data needed:** contract price terms, price-change effective dates, customer-level price history.

### R4. Address Home Appliances costs
- **Observed:** unit costs above target cost –$2.6M of gross profit; margin 29.41% vs 33.96% planned.
- **Impact:** Home Appliances gross profit –$5.2M vs target.
- **Possible mechanism (hypothesis):** a step-up in freight or component costs that was not reflected in prices.
- **Action:** split the cost increase into its drivers, renegotiate where possible, and reflect the remainder in the next price review.
- **Success measure:** Home Appliances gross margin back within 1 point of target.
- **Data needed:** cost breakdown by component, freight and supplier.

### R5. Fix the monthly phasing of targets
- **Observed:** 4 months beat target and they are the seasonal peaks; most other months fell short. The target phasing is flatter than the sales pattern.
- **Impact:** monthly variances are inflated in both directions, which hides the real trend and triggers false alarms.
- **Possible mechanism (hypothesis):** annual targets were spread with an average seasonal profile that understates the holiday peak.
- **Action:** phase each category's target from its own historical monthly share of the year.
- **Success measure:** monthly phasing gaps within ±0.2 percentage points and tracking signal within ±4.
- **Data needed:** at least three years of monthly history per category.

## 5. What this data cannot tell us

- Why customers bought less in a region (market, competitor or distribution) - there is no market or competitor data.
- Whether discounted volume was incremental or pulled forward - there is no baseline demand.
- Whether targets were too ambitious or execution was weak - the comparison with a naive forecast shows where targets were far from history, not why.
- What drove the cost increase - unit cost is a single number per line.

## 6. Data quality behind these numbers

- The raw file had 21,815 rows. Cleaning removed 525 exact duplicates and 25 blank rows, merged 252 duplicate copies, held 113 rows for review and delivered 20,900 clean lines.
- 4,672 values were repaired. Every repair was exact (solved from Sales = Qty × Price, Gross profit = Sales − Qty × Cost, the Line_ID key or the product master); nothing was estimated. Each one is listed with its original value in `data/processed/Cleaned_Sales_Data.xlsx`.
- Sensitivity: excluding every line with a repaired value, sales variance would be –1.92% and gross-profit variance –6.40% (all lines: –2.04% and –6.53%). The 113 rows held for review carry $2.9M of target sales (0.54% of the total) and are excluded from every figure.
